1. Hook: frame 0 is the crowded honey label already lit, with a red name touching both edges, a green badge and a blue banner, rung in red as the voice says "too much" | Why: the viewer sees the mess in the first second, before any word explains it
2. Pacing curve: a change every 1.5-2 s; fastest at 0-2 s (two rings), a held pause of 1.5 s on the finished label at 17.5-19 s | Why: the fixes land fast and the clean label gets time to be looked at
3. Camera: a push to 1.09 before fix 1 (room), 1.08 before fix 2 (the name), a pull back for fix 3 and the before/after | Why: each fix is seen close, then the whole label is judged
4. Light and mood: a warm lamp on a dark green shop wall and a wooden shelf, amber marks every tool action, the label goes from busy to calm as the mood score brightens | Why: amber means 'the tool is working', so the label itself carries the change
5. Type motion: one title line per fix (Fix 1 give it room) and chips that pop on the word, the accent word in amber in the subtitles | Why: each fix has a name the eye can hold
6. Transitions: no cuts; the camera pushes and pulls and the label morphs in place; the before flashes back at the payoff | Why: one continuous object, so the change reads as cause and effect
7. Sound moments: opening sting 0 s, a stamp on 'cheap', a stack of soft lands as the clutter leaves, a drag as the name shrinks, two clicks of the eyedropper, the reveal chord on 'premium' | Why: every change is heard on its word
8. Rewatch moment: the name shrinking and spreading its letters at about 9-10 s while the line below it follows | Why: the quiet-to-expensive switch is the surprise people scrub back for
9. Payoff: the label holds fully lit with the old busy version as a tilted thumbnail, two ticks, then loops back to the crowded frame 0 | Why: the viewer keeps the before and the after side by side to copy
