## 1. What makes a voice sound natural (and what ours did)
Findings (vendor guides for speech models; they agree with each other, and none is a study: treat them as craft notes):
- Write for the ear: short sentences, but a mix of lengths; a script of only short clipped lines sounds robotic, a mix sounds human. Contractions ("it's", "don't") make a clear difference. (Google Cloud, Chirp 3 HD "crafting natural scripts"; Noiz "write for the ear"; Sarvam TTS best practices.)
- Punctuation is the pause: a comma is a short breath, a full stop a full one; under-punctuated text sounds rushed. (Deepgram Aura-2 formatting guide; Google Cloud.)
- Read it aloud before rendering; fragments and labels were not covered by any guide, so we test them by ear. (Christy Tucker, voice-over script style tips.)
- Our pipeline (scripts/showreel/voice.py, Kokoro, voice af_heart, speed 1): with `voice.gap` each sentence is spoken on its own and followed by that much silence; without it the whole text is one take. Every design config had no gap and a script of labels ("Fix one: darken the photo. Fix two: a solid band."): 9-11 sentences of 2-5 words in 14 s. That is the list-like, clipped delivery the owner heard, and with no breath a line was still being said when the next change began.
- Sound under speech: effects sit about a third of the voice's level and the bed ducks under it (hyperframes steer.md note; our DT.score duck 7 dB). Kept; the new flip and proof sounds follow it.
Decision: rules-c3.md V1-V3.

## 2. What makes a "before" believable
Findings (design teaching sources, listicles and one textbook chapter; no measurements):
- The common beginner mistakes are ordinary, not extreme: too many fonts, filling every space, too much content, no focal point ("if everything screams, nothing is heard"), decoration before purpose. (Adobe Express, common graphic design mistakes; Noble Desktop, 12 common mistakes; G2 expert roundup; Nielsen Norman Group, junior designer mistakes.)
- A makeover should say whether it is a makeover or a small fix, and start from what the piece must do. (O'Reilly, Design to Sell, chapter 13.)
- Pattern from design-fix reels we have studied (patterns only): the before is a real-looking draft with ONE mistake, shown plainly, and the viewer recognises their own work in it.
Decision: rules-c3.md B1-B3.
